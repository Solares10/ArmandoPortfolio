Shock Tube Analysis and CFD Validation

Graduate research, Cal Poly Pomona, Aug 2025 to May 2026 Under 0.5% error vs. theory, all six configurations

I used Rankine–Hugoniot relations to characterize shock tube dynamics for six driver gases (CH₄, air, N₂, CO₂, H₂ and He) driving into methane. I built a MATLAB solver for thermodynamic states, wave speeds and post-interaction flow, then validated ANSYS Fluent simulations against it using x–t diagrams.

One finding: CO₂ produced more secondary reflected-shock interactions than the other heavy gases because of its higher acoustic impedance.

  • ANSYS Fluent
  • MATLAB
  • Compressible flow
  • Shock waves

Humphrey and Brayton Cycle Analysis

Airbreathing propulsion, January to May 2024

I compared the two thermodynamic cycles to show how pressure-gain combustion improves efficiency. Parametric studies of efficiency and net work, with isentropic losses added, showed the Humphrey cycle reaching higher thermal efficiency at low pressure ratios.

  • MATLAB
  • Thermodynamics
  • Propulsion

Earth-to-Mars Trajectory Analysis

Orbital mechanics, August to December 2023

I processed ephemeris data with Lambert’s method to find departure and arrival velocities, then made pork chop plots mapping launch windows, arrival dates and C3 energy for different mission scenarios.
